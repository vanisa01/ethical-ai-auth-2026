Why this matters now

Code now operates at social scale

  • Web systems shape attention, beliefs, relationships, and civic life.
  • AI personalizes what people see, believe, buy, and trust.
  • Innovation now moves faster than society can absorb its consequences.

In older software eras, bugs mostly blocked tasks. Today, product decisions can affect mental health, misinformation, polarization, and the quality of public discourse.

Scale

One ranking decision can shape millions of daily impressions.

Speed

Product iteration is continuous while ethical reflection is often delayed.

Risk

Optimization without ethics becomes social engineering by default.

References

Baumeister and Vohs, 2018. Reuters Institute Digital News Report, 2023.

Social media

What platforms actually optimize

Infinite scroll Streaks Push alerts Variable rewards

Most large platforms reward attention capture because advertising business models reward time-on-platform. The result is a conflict: what keeps users engaged is not always what helps them think clearly or live well.

A feed ranking algorithm may maximize clicks by prioritizing emotionally intense content, even when that increases anxiety or misinformation.
References

Eyal, 2014. Harris TED Talk, 2016. Alter, 2017.

The Social Dilemma

If you are not paying, you are also being trained

  1. Prediction systems learn from behavior.
  2. Optimization systems shape what users do next.
  3. Over time, design patterns can influence habit, identity, and public discourse.

The ethical problem is not only harmful content. It is behavior engineering at scale, often without meaningful consent.

Core claim

Platforms do not only predict behavior. They can steer behavior.

Documentary, popular criticism, and platform testimony all converge on the same concern: optimization can become manipulation.
References

Orlowski, 2020. Lanier, 2018. Williams, 2018.

A philosophical lens

Aristotle's question for modern platforms

Aristotle helps us ask one practical question: what kind of habits are our systems training in people?

Habits shape character

If a platform trains constant checking, outrage, or dependency, that is already an ethical outcome.

Design trains habits

Notifications, feeds, and ranking systems do not just serve users. They also teach users how to behave.

Good design supports self-control

Ethical programming should strengthen autonomy, reflection, and informed choice instead of compulsion.

The real question

Are we building systems that help people flourish, or systems that keep them reactive and distracted?

References

Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics. Vallor, 2016.

Ethical programming

Not just legality, but design responsibility

  • Respect user autonomy
  • Minimize foreseeable harm
  • Make tradeoffs explicit
  • Protect privacy by default
  • Preserve fairness and dignity
  • Keep teams accountable
◍

Ask who benefits

Every product decision creates winners, losers, and externalized costs.

◐

Ask who is pressured

Behavioral nudges become unethical when they exploit vulnerability.

◎

Ask what becomes normal

Repeated defaults become culture, and culture becomes infrastructure.

References

ACM Code of Ethics, 2018. Floridi and Taddeo, 2016. Vallor, 2016.

Ethical AI

Where AI systems go wrong

Bias

Models replicate unequal data and unequal history.

Opacity

Users cannot contest decisions they cannot understand.

Manipulation

Personalization can target weakness rather than support goals.

Surveillance

Data collection expands far beyond legitimate need.

AI systems inherit both the strengths and the prejudices of the data, institutions, and incentives around them.
Ethical AI requires explanation, contestability, human oversight, and limits on automated power.
References

Buolamwini and Gebru, 2018. Selbst and Barocas, 2019. Mittelstadt et al., 2016.

Practical framework

The five-question ethical check

1

Who benefits?

2

Who might be harmed?

3

What behavior are we incentivizing?

4

Can users understand and control what happens?

5

Could we publicly defend this design tomorrow?

This can be used in sprint planning, PR review, design critique, or launch readiness.

References

Gillespie, 2018. Stark and Hoey, 2021.

Coding example

Non-ethical code vs ethical code

Dark-pattern notification logic
def send_notification(user_id, message):
    notification = {
        "priority": "high",
        "sound": True,
        "vibration": True,
    }
    send_time = calculate_when_user_most_vulnerable()
    schedule_notification(notification, send_time)
    track_engagement_for_manipulation(user_id)

Overrides user settings, targets weak moments, tracks behavior for manipulation.

User-respecting notification logic
def send_notification(user_id, message):
    prefs = get_user_notification_preferences(user_id)
    if not prefs["notifications_enabled"]:
        return {"status": "skipped"}
    if is_in_user_quiet_hours(user_id):
        return {"status": "deferred"}
    schedule_notification(message, prefs["preferred_send_time"])
    log_notification_sent(user_id, reason="user_request")

Respects consent, quiet hours, user timing, and transparency.

Ethical code is often more explicit because it encodes values like consent, transparency, and user control.
References

ACM Code of Ethics, 2018. GDPR. IEEE 7000-2021.

For web programming students

What we can do now

  • Use privacy-preserving defaults.
  • Add friction to risky actions and stopping cues to endless flows.
  • Explain recommendation logic in user-friendly language.
  • Track harm signals, not only growth metrics.
  • Add an ethics section to project readmes and code reviews.
01

Consent before collection

02

Explain why content appears

03

Design for stopping, not compulsion

04

Test bias and abuse cases
